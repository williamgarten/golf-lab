Film a putt from directly above. Golf Lab measures your start line, ball speed, green speed and, with two stickers on your putter, stroke path, face angle and tempo.

Live camera with a ball marker, putt direction and level. It talks you through the setup and records by itself.

On the 0.5× lens it sees about twice as much (2 ft high ≈ 5½ ft), but the edges bend a little — keep the putt line through the middle.

Position

  • Phone upright, long side along the putt line, straight above the line.
  • Ball about a quarter of the way up from the back edge, so the backstroke fits behind it.
  • Level: Settings → Camera → turn on Grid and Level. Pointing straight down, the camera shows a + that lines up when it’s flat.
  • Mount: an overhead phone arm / boom tripod (~$20–40), or a selfie stick clamped to a chair.
  • Hole doesn’t fit? Put a tee ~3 ft down your line, tap the tee, and type the hole distance.

Video

  • 1080p at 60 fps (Settings → Camera → Record Video). Slo-mo isn’t needed.
  • Keep your shadow off the ball and stickers.
  • Green speed needs 3+ ft of roll in the picture on a flat-ish putt.

Putter stickers (for stroke path, face angle, tempo)

  • Two neon pink or orange dot stickers (¾ in office dots), on top of the putter head: one at the toe, one at the heel, as far apart as possible.
  • Not green, yellow or white — those get confused with the grass or the ball.
  • One sticker works too (path and tempo, no face angle).

Clock drills

Film yourself from the front. Golf Lab reads where your lead arm stops going back and going through, like a clock hand: arm straight down is 6:00, level with the ground is 9:00. Hit 9 to 9, 7:30 to 7:30… and build your own yardage chart.

Through is counted as a mirror image: “9 to 9” = arm level with the ground on both sides (some coaches call it 9 to 3).

The camera watches every swing and tells you the positions out loud. No taps between shots.

Camera setup for wedges
  • In front of you, square to your chest (on the ball line, looking at you) — about 10 ft away.
  • Phone sideways (landscape) at waist height, on a normal tripod — no overhead arm needed.
  • Your whole body, head to feet, in the picture, with room above your head for the club.
  • Phone straight up and down (not tilted) — the app checks this. A tilted phone makes 9:00 read wrong.
  • A plain background and no one else in the picture helps the body tracker.
  • The first time, the body tracker (~10 MB) downloads. After that it works offline.
